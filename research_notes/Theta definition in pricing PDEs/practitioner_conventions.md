# Practitioner / Market Conventions for Option Theta vs Model (PDE) Theta

Scope note: Sources found online were mainly practitioner columns (Wystup/MathFinance, Ballabio/Implementing QuantLib, Moontower), vendor glossaries, and EU regulatory text (EBA RTS on backtesting/PLA). I could not reach the full text of Natenberg, Taleb, Sinclair, Clark, Bergomi (beyond chapter-1 notes), or Bloomberg OVML help, so I do not attribute specific claims to those books. See Gaps.

## 1. What is held fixed when theta is computed: sticky strike vs sticky delta, implied vol vs instantaneous variance, vol-surface roll-down

### Takeaway
"Theta" is not a single quantity. Practitioners say the number depends on what you hold fixed when you move the clock: market quotes, zero rates, forwards, implied vol per strike, or implied vol per delta. Vendor and practitioner sources treat the vol-surface "roll" (an option moving to a shorter tenor and a different smile point on a static surface) as a separate P&L item from Black-Scholes theta, which holds implied vol constant. Some practitioners add the roll to theta and call the total "effective" or "shadow" theta.

### Cited Findings
- **Documented (QuantLib practitioner):** Numerical theta is bump-and-reprice: change "the time to maturity (which, in the case of QuantLib, means moving the evaluation date)". Theta per day is new price minus old price, with no annualisation. — [Ballabio, Implementing QuantLib: "Different approaches to numerical Theta"](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)
- **Documented, with example:** Ballabio gives three ways to "keep everything else the same" when rolling a floating-rate bond's evaluation date forward: (1) same market quotes, re-bootstrapped (theta ≈ -0.00719); (2) same zero rates, curve rigidly translated (≈ -0.00709), which "won't reprice the input swaps exactly"; (3) same forward rates via ImpliedTermStructure (≈ -0.00720). — [Implementing QuantLib](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)
- **Opinion (Ballabio):** On whether one correct theta exists: "I'm not sure there is one"; "any choice will likely be a trade-off"; "the blanket is short". — [Implementing QuantLib](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)
- **Documented, example from a QuantLib user:** In that example the theta bump moves to the next *business* day (Friday to Monday, 3 calendar days), yet the result is still called "theta per day". — [Implementing QuantLib](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)
- **Definitions (practitioner):** Under sticky strike, "IV at each strike stays constant"; under sticky delta, "the vol of the 'floating' delta strikes remains unchanged, so effectively we 'ride' the vol smile". — [Moontower, Connecting Vol Surfaces to Option P/L](https://moontowermeta.com/connecting-vol-surfaces-to-option-p-l/)
- **Vendor glossary:** Vol-surface roll is how a position's implied vol changes "purely from the passage of time as contracts slide along the volatility surface". Traders update roll estimates daily under sticky-strike or sticky-delta assumptions, "each of which embeds a different hypothesis about how the surface will move". Roll calculations assume a static surface shape, "which is almost never empirically valid". — [Convex Trade glossary: Vol Surface Roll](https://convextrade.com/glossary/vol-surface-roll)
- **Vendor glossary:** Theta measures the loss of extrinsic premium as time passes *with implied volatility held constant*. Term-structure roll is the separate P&L from an option moving to a shorter tenor on a non-flat vol surface. — [Convex Trade glossary: IV term structure roll](https://convextrade.com/glossary/implied-volatility-term-structure-roll)
- **Vendor glossary:** A steep upward-sloping term structure gives short-vol positions positive roll income. An inverted term structure can produce negative roll that "overwhelms theta". A 3M 25-delta put rolling to 1M "may find itself on a steeper part of the skew curve". — [Convex Trade glossary: Vol Surface Roll](https://convextrade.com/glossary/vol-surface-roll)
- **Opinion/practice (blog):** A longer-dated option's vol roll-down is treated as "shadow theta": "its effective theta will be its Black Scholes theta + shadow theta". — [Moontower, Shadow theta](https://moontowermeta.com/shadow-theta/)
- **Empirical (fund study):** A study of equity index surfaces found "Sticky Surface" best for short (1w–3m) and medium (6m–2y) maturities, sticky strike a close second, and sticky delta best for long maturities on APAC indices. This bears on which roll assumption is realistic. — [Coremont, Equity implied volatility dynamics](https://www.coremont.com/news-insights/equity-implied-volatility-dynamics/)
- **Theory (Bergomi, via chapter notes):** In Black-Scholes the hedged P&L is −½S²Γ(δS²/S² − σ̂²δt). The implied vol σ̂ is "a parameter ... to control how the gamma and theta terms for the spot offset each other". P&L from changes in parameters "that were supposed to stay constant in the pricing model" is mark-to-market P&L, so in Black-Scholes a change in σ̂ generates MtM P&L. Stochastic vol models must produce additional theta terms. — [Notes on Bergomi, Stochastic Volatility Modeling, Ch. 1](https://junfanz1.github.io/blog/book%20notes%20series/Stochastic-Volatility-Modeling-Char-1-Introduction-Notes); see also [Bergomi, Smile Dynamics IV (Risk 2009)](https://www.maths.univ-evry.fr/pages_perso/crepey/Finance/0904_tech_bergomi.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Model (PDE) theta" ∂V/∂t holds all state variables of the model fixed. In Black-Scholes that state is (S, σ̂, r, q), so the model theta equals the "implied vol held fixed, spot held fixed" trader theta. In a local-vol or stochastic-vol model, the PDE theta holds the instantaneous variance or local-vol surface fixed in calendar time. Desk theta usually holds the *implied* surface fixed in some coordinate (strike, or delta/moneyness, and usually expiry date). The two differ by the roll-down of implied vol. This follows from the Convex Trade split (theta at constant IV vs roll) together with Bergomi's point that implied-vol changes are MtM P&L. No single source states this mapping explicitly.
- Sticky-strike theta keeps σ_imp(K, T_expiry) fixed. Sticky-delta/moneyness theta keeps σ_imp(K/F, T) fixed. The two differ on a skewed surface even with spot unchanged, because the forward drifts with carry over the step and so moneyness changes. This is my inference; no source gives a worked comparison.

### Gaps
- I found no documentation from Bloomberg OVML, Numerix, FINCAD or OpenGamma saying whether their theta holds implied vol fixed per strike, per delta, or per expiry date, or whether it rolls down the surface. Searches returned no vendor help pages.
- I found no published worked example comparing sticky-strike and sticky-delta theta numerically.

## 2. Carry/forward drift: spot fixed vs forward fixed; "theta with carry" vs "pure theta"

### Takeaway
How theta treats carry is a documented source of disagreement between systems. Wystup shows a real USD/JPY case where one system's one-day reprice theta (spot fixed, trade date moved) has the opposite sign from another system's analytic theta. He also shows that theta has a vol-time part and a separate rates/delivery-time part, and that the forward-curve part is "the dominating one on average".

### Cited Findings
- **Documented example (Wystup, MathFinance, Oct 2022):** The example is a USD call/JPY put struck at 107, spot 127.00 on 26 May 2022, 6M expiry, with a downward-sloping USD/JPY forward (USD rate 1.77%, JPY rate −0.40%). In SuperDerivatives the option is worth less than intrinsic, so one would expect positive theta, but the system shows **negative** theta of JPY −1,894. Moving the trade date one day "keeping all other market and contract values the same" does lower the value (18,890,895 to 18,889,350), so the reported theta matches a reprice. Eikon shows theta **+4,064 JPY** for the same option. Wystup says Eikon's theta "does not seem to consider the business day calendar but is more likely derived from a mathematical derivative". — [Wystup, "How a Long Call Option can be Long Gamma, Long Theta and Short Theta", MathFinance FX Column](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)
- **Documented definitions (Wystup):** "this is the notion of theta a trader usually thinks of: what happens to the position on the next day, assuming market variables don't change. As a quant one might rather think of theta being the derivative of the option value with respect to running time. This will yield a change per year." — [Wystup, MathFinance](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)
- **Documented (Wystup):** The FX Black-Scholes formula uses two time spans: t for volatility (trade date to expiry) and T for discounting (spot date to delivery). In the example, t shrinks by one day on the next trade date but T does not change, because the US Memorial Day holiday leaves spot and delivery dates unchanged. The theta shown by SuperDerivatives "only shows the impact of the 'optionality' but not the impact caused by the forward curve, which is actually the dominating one on average." Formula reference: Wystup, *FX Options and Structured Products*, 2nd ed., Wiley 2017, p. 29. — [Wystup, MathFinance](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)
- **Documented (Wystup) conclusions:** "Any theta figure should come with a clear definition what it means." Different systems can give different answers for the same market and option, "even for standard options". He states this "is relevant for P&L-Explain". — [Wystup, MathFinance](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)
- **Documented (Wystup):** In-the-money options can have negative time value when the forward curve is in backwardation, and "in FX markets, this is quite common". — [Wystup, MathFinance](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)
- **Documented (curve analogue):** Holding zero rates fixed, holding forwards fixed, or holding market quotes fixed give different thetas (see Section 1). This is the rates version of the spot-fixed vs forward-fixed choice. — [Implementing QuantLib](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)

### Inferences
- With spot held fixed and the date rolled, theta includes the carry drift: the forward F = S·e^{(r−q)τ} moves toward spot, and discounting accretes. With the forward held fixed, theta is "pure" optionality/vol decay plus discount accretion. In the Black-Scholes PDE written in spot, ∂V/∂t (spot fixed) = rV − (r−q)S·Δ − ½σ²S²Γ. So the spot-fixed theta contains the carry term −(r−q)SΔ, and the forward-fixed theta does not. This is standard algebra, not a sourced convention, and the Wystup example matches it.
- "Theta with carry" vs "pure theta" as named labels: I found no vendor document that uses those exact terms.

### Gaps
- Which convention Bloomberg OVML, OpenGamma Strata, Numerix and FINCAD use by default (spot vs forward fixed) is not documented in anything I could reach.
- I could not access Clark's *Foreign Exchange Option Pricing* text on theta and carry. The book exists (Wiley 2011) and includes a figure on "FX trading dates of importance" ([O'Reilly front matter](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/foreign-exchange-option/9781119978602/OEBPS/9781119978602_fm3.htm)), but its content is unverified here.

## 3. Business-day vs calendar-day theta, weekends/holidays, trading-time / variance-time and event weighting

### Takeaway
Divisors differ by system: theta/365 (calendar) is the most common display convention, and theta/252 (trading days) is also used. FX and equity desks use weighted-day "variance time" clocks, where weekends and holidays get fractional weight and event days get extra weight. Under those clocks, one-day theta is uneven and differs from annual theta divided by 365. Weekend decay is a documented empirical issue.

### Cited Findings
- **Documented (Wystup):** "In practice, however, there are all the matters around trade days, business day, holidays, and currency pair specific conventions. One can't just calculate an annual change, divide by 365 and expect to get the same answer." Analytic Black-Scholes theta shows "an average daily change of the option value ignoring the up- and down jumps of theta when business days are considered in detail. Both approaches might be useful to look at." — [Wystup, MathFinance](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)
- **Documented (FX date mechanics, Wystup):** A holiday can leave expiry and delivery dates unchanged from one trade date to the next. The vol-time t then shrinks while the rates-time T does not, so theta jumps around holidays. — [Wystup, MathFinance](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)
- **Documented (QuantLib API):** `OneAssetOption` exposes `theta()` (annualised) and `thetaPerDay()`. — [QuantLib thetaPerDay man page](https://www.systutorials.com/?p=336690); [PyQL theta_per_day](https://docs.enthought.com/pyql/_autosummary/quantlib.instruments.vanillaoption.VanillaOption.theta_per_day.html). *Not verified from source:* that thetaPerDay = theta/365. The search tool's summary also flagged this as unconfirmed.
- **Documented (R package convention):** "CallTheta or PutTheta can be divided by 365 to get Theta per calendar day or by 252 to get Theta by trading day." — [ustreasuries R package, ThetaCall](https://rdrr.io/github/grfiv/ustreasuries/man/ThetaCall.html)
- **Practitioner claim:** Dividing by 365 is "the most common convention; some platforms divide by 252". — [Ryan O'Connell Finance, Option Theta](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/option-theta/) (secondary source)
- **Practitioner (FX short-dated pricing):** A one-week option is often treated as five business days plus a fractional allowance for each weekend day, for the risk of a shock between Friday's NY close and Monday's Sydney open. Calendar vol (the quoted measure) can be converted to business-day vol because the premium must be the same under either. — [GlobalCapital/Derivatives Week, "Pricing Short Dated Options"](https://www.globalcapital.com/article/28mwtvkodfvd095dm36yo/derivatives/pricing-short-dated-options) (from search snippet; full text not retrieved)
- **Practitioner opinion/model (Moontower):** Calendar-day models (365, like VIX) and business-day models (~251) disagree. "The weekend effect is a mathematical artifact that presents itself as 'implied vol increases on Monday'." Moontower proposes a blended ~308-day clock with weekend/holiday days weighted 0.5 and overnight weighted 0.25, which he calls "in the right ballpark", so Fri PM to Mon AM = 1.25 days vs 2.25 under calendar time. Options "typically do not decay as much as the model's theta would predict" over weekends. — [Moontower, Weekend theta](https://moontowermeta.com/weekend-theta/); [Moontower, A Variance Schedule](https://notion.moontowermeta.com/a-variance-schedule); [Moontower, Understanding Variance Time](https://notion.moontowermeta.com/understanding-variance-time)
- **Empirical (cited via practitioner summary):** Fama (1965) found Friday-close to Monday-close variance only ~22% above other days, and French (1980) ~19%. Calendar time would imply ~3x, so the data sit closer to trading time. — search summary of [core.ac.uk paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/14919935.pdf) / [MenthorQ](https://menthorq.com/guide/the-hidden-problem-with-theta/) (secondary; figures not checked against the originals)
- **Empirical (academic, via blog):** Jones & Shemesh (1996–2007 data, AFA 2010) found long equity-option portfolios lose ~0.62% over weekends, which the post attributes to "market makers are not correctly adjusting the implied volatilities on Fridays". They found no similar effect in index options. Euan Sinclair commented that the effect "might not be tradeable". — [Talton CM blog, "Theta and weekends again"](https://taltoncm.blogspot.com/2017/08/theta-and-weekends-again.html)
- **Empirical (data vendor):** In an OptionMetrics backtest of a 1DTE ATM put-write strategy, excluding weekend-spanning trades cut cumulative return from 28.07% to 8.94%. — [OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/?p=2992) (from search summary)
- **Practitioner:** Comparing total implied variance across expiries can reveal event risk in a short-dated expiry. Check the vendor's time convention. — [MQL5 blog, Reading FX options](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/777056) (low-authority source)

### Inferences
- Under a weighted-day clock, vol-time τ_v is the weighted count of remaining days. A one-day theta reprice on Friday moves τ_v by the weekend weight (e.g. 1 + 2w), not by 3/365, so desk theta is uneven while the PDE ∂V/∂t in calendar time is smooth. The model theta can be recovered as ∂V/∂τ_v · dτ_v/dt.
- Whether a desk books Friday's theta as one business day of decay or as the decay through to Monday is a local policy choice. The QuantLib example's business-day roll and Wystup's trade-date roll are two documented instances.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Natenberg's (*Option Volatility and Pricing*), Taleb's (*Dynamic Hedging*) or Sinclair's (*Volatility Trading* / *Option Trading*) exact positions on weekend theta or trading-day counts. These books are widely said to discuss the topic, but no quotable text was reached.
- Bloomberg OVML's day-count and weekend weighting for theta (and its "overnight" theta definition) is not documented publicly in anything I found.
- Clark's and Wystup's book treatment of event vols and weighted days in FX vol-time was not retrieved.

## 4. Multi-day theta: full repricing V(τ−h) − V(τ) vs integrated PDE theta; theta in P&L explain and FRTB P&L attribution

### Takeaway
Practitioners compute theta by moving the evaluation/trade date and fully repricing with market data frozen. Over h days this gives V(t+h, frozen state) − V(t), not h × instantaneous theta. Regulators require the passage-of-time ("theta") effect to be in actual P&L for backtesting, and to be treated consistently between hypothetical P&L, risk-theoretical P&L, and the ES/stress measures. In P&L explain, the order in which time is moved changes how the theta term is split out.

### Cited Findings
- **Documented (practice):** One-day theta is computed by "mov[ing] the trade date ... to the next day, keeping all other market and contract values the same". — [Wystup, MathFinance](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf); equivalently, "moving the evaluation date" in QuantLib — [Implementing QuantLib](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)
- **Practitioner:** Traders on a Friday "keep their vols the same and 'roll their dates' forward" to model weekend decay. — [Moontower, Pricing 0DTEs / money-math archive](https://moontowermeta.com/category/newsletter-thoughts/the-money-angle/money-math/page/5/) (from search summary)
- **Regulatory, EU (documented):** EBA Final Draft RTS on backtesting and PLA (EBA/RTS/2020/02), Art. 3(1): hypothetical changes in value "shall be computed using the same pricing methods, model parametrisations, market data and any other technique as those used in the end-of-day valuation process, without considering any fees and commissions." Art. 3(2): "Institutions shall reflect the passage of time effect in the hypothetical changes in the trading desk portfolio's value consistently with the treatment they apply in relation to such effect in the calculation of the expected shortfall risk measure ... and in the calculation of the stress scenario risk measure." — [EBA Final Draft RTS on Backtesting and PLA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library//EBA-RTS-2020-02%20Final%20draft%20RTS%20on%20Backtesting%20and%20PLA%20requirements.pdf)
- **Regulatory, EU (rationale text):** "The institution should capture the passage of time (i.e. the theta effect) in the APL and should reflect the same effect in the context of the HPL consistently with the treatment the institution applies for capturing this effect in the calculation of the expected shortfall and ... stress scenario risk measure." — [EBA RTS](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library//EBA-RTS-2020-02%20Final%20draft%20RTS%20on%20Backtesting%20and%20PLA%20requirements.pdf)
- **Regulatory, EU (consultation feedback):** A respondent argued the theta effect should be excluded from backtesting because theta-negative desks would breach repeatedly. EBA's reply: "The Basel standards require that the time effect must be included in the back testing ... the time effect is already included in the APL by definition, since institutions are requested to revalue the portfolio on the subsequent day ... for HPL, institutions are required to reflect risk factors, including the time effect, consistently with the methodology used in the expected shortfall, where the portfolio is assumed to be static." — [EBA RTS](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library//EBA-RTS-2020-02%20Final%20draft%20RTS%20on%20Backtesting%20and%20PLA%20requirements.pdf); industry concern on "theta impact on Actual P&L back-testing" also raised in the [Industry Response to EBA RTS](https://www.eba.europa.eu/system/files/webform/documents/ddm-fileupload/2981938/1.0/view_uploadFiles/Industry-Response-to-EBA-RTS_Backtesting_PLA_Final.pdf)
- **Regulatory, Basel (via search summary):** MAR10 defines HPL as the daily P&L from revaluing the previous day's end-of-day positions with the current day's end-of-day market data. APL includes intraday trading, time effects, and new/modified deals, but excludes fees/commissions. "Time effects should be treated in a consistent manner in the HPL and risk-theoretical P&L." — [BCBS Basel Framework MAR10 (SIF copy)](https://www.sif.admin.ch/dam/sif/de/dokumente/basel-3/MAR10.pdf.download.pdf/MAR10.pdf) (link returned 404 when fetched; wording taken from search snippet, verify against [bis.org Basel Framework MAR](https://www.bis.org/basel_framework/chapter/MAR/32.htm))
- **Educational (FRTB PLA):** RTPL as a sensitivity-based prediction includes a Theta × Δt term. In a worked example, theta of −$150K/day × 1 day = −$0.15M. — [AcadiFi, P&L attribution RTPL vs actual](https://www.acadifi.com/community/pnl-attribution-risk-theoretical-vs-actual) (educational, not primary)
- **Practitioner (P&L explain):** Sensitivity-based explain splits P&L into delta, gamma, vega, volga, theta and an unexplained residual. Whether time is moved first or last "is often the biggest methodological decision". In sequential explain it changes the repricing state for later moves, and cross terms go to whichever factor moves second. Taylor explain breaks down on large-move days, where full reprice is recommended. — search summaries of [Atoti/ActiveViam PnL Explain docs](https://docs.activeviam.com/products/solutions/mra/6.0/online-help/calculations/pnl-explain.html) and related sources (the Atoti page returned HTTP 500 on direct fetch; treat attribution of the "biggest methodological decision" quote as unverified)
- **Academic (thesis):** In a comparison, full-revaluation attribution left zero unexplained, while risk-based (Taylor) attribution left a residual of −58 on P&L of ≈ −238,600. The author recommends risk-based attribution with a threshold on the unexplained. — [Li Shengxia, CEU thesis 2020](https://www.etd.ceu.edu/2020/li_shengxia.pdf) (from search summary)
- **Practitioner opinion:** P&L attribution practice varies widely across banks ("the P&L attribution mess"). — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/risk-management/market-risk/2466633/the-pl-attribution-mess) (paywalled; title only)

### Inferences
- **Slide vs integrated theta (mathematical, not sourced):** If the state x (spot or forward, implied surface in its chosen coordinates, curves) is held frozen, then V(t+h, x) − V(t, x) = ∫₀ʰ ∂_tV(t+s, x) ds exactly. So multi-day "slide" theta equals the integral of the instantaneous theta *along the frozen-state path*. It differs from h·θ(t) by the term structure of theta (½h²·∂²V/∂t² + …), which matters near expiry and over weekends or holidays where theta jumps. It also differs from the PDE theta integrated along the *realised* path, which is what appears in the hedged-P&L identity ∫(θ + ½Γσ²S²)dt.
- The slide only matches the model PDE theta if the "frozen" coordinates match the model's state variables. Freezing implied vol per strike in a local-vol model is not the same as freezing the local-vol surface. Freezing spot is not the same as freezing the forward. The vol-time and rates-time clocks must also step in the same way as the model clock. Wystup's t vs T example and Ballabio's three curve-freezing choices both show the frozen-state choice moving the number.
- The EBA wording ("portfolio is assumed to be static" in ES; revalue on the subsequent day for APL) implies regulators see theta as the full-revaluation date roll with a static portfolio, not as an analytic derivative. The RTPL/HPL consistency rule exists because those definitions can differ between systems.
