# Model theta freezes the state, desk theta freezes quotes

Yes. In the pricing-PDE literature, theta is the partial derivative of option value with respect to time, with every other state variable and input held fixed. In Black-Scholes that means S, σ, r and q. In Heston it means S, the instantaneous variance v, r, q and the model parameters. It is written ∂V/∂t, or equivalently −∂V/∂τ with τ = T − t. Your notebook's definition matches this standard. Its breakeven split (gamma, vega/drift, vanna, volga, carry summing to theta) is the Heston PDE rearranged term by term. Your multi-day theta V(τ−h) − V(τ) with S and v frozen is exactly the integral of instantaneous PDE theta along that frozen-state path, so horizon breakevens built as integrals of the instantaneous breakevens add up exactly. Where your notebook departs from common practice is in conventions, not in the mathematics. Practitioner theta usually freezes the *implied* vol surface (sticky strike or sticky delta), not the instantaneous variance. Some desks report theta net of carry and some report it bare. Day-count divisors (252 vs 365) and weekend treatment vary from system to system. Your quantity is a legitimate, internally consistent "model theta". It will not equal a vendor's or a trading desk's "theta" without translation, and one practitioner states the general lesson plainly: "any theta figure should come with a clear definition what it means" ([Wystup](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)).

A note on sources: several canonical texts (Hull's book pages, Wilmott, Rouah, Taleb, Sinclair, Bergomi's book, the text layer of Heston 1993) could not be read directly. The report attributes formulas only to sources that were actually read, and it labels secondary summaries and derivations made for this report as such.

## Every verified reference defines theta as a partial with the state frozen

The verified definitions agree. Hull's chapter slides define theta as "the rate of change of the value with respect to the passage of time" ([Hull Ch. 17 slides, snippet](https://issuu.com/zalimkasap/docs/ch17hullofod7thed)). The fuller book wording, "with all else remaining the same", is widely quoted but could not be checked against a page. The NYU Derivative Securities notes keyed to Hull state that "theta is defined as the derivative with respect to a decrease in time to option expiry" and call this "the standard market convention" ([Kohn & Allen](https://math.nyu.edu/~kohn/derivative.securities/2012/section5.pdf)). Wikipedia writes **Θ = −∂V/∂τ**, with τ in years ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_(finance))). For Heston specifically, NAG's Greeks routine defines theta as **−∂P/∂T** with T the time to expiry. Spot, v_t, r, q and the parameters (κ, η, σ_v, ρ) are separate inputs with their own sensitivities, which makes theta a partial with all of them fixed ([NAG s30ndf](https://support.nag.com/numeric/nl/nagdoc_29/flhtml/s/s30ndf.html)).

The derivation of the SV valuation equation makes the same point structurally. Gatheral's lecture notes (the precursor of *The Volatility Surface*, explicitly following Wilmott) treat V(S, v, t) as a function of **independent variables S, v and t**, apply Itô, and hedge out dS and dv. The Heston case of the resulting PDE is ∂V/∂t + ½vS²V_SS + ρηvSV_Sv + ½η²vV_vv + rSV_S − rV = λ′(v − v̄′)V_v ([Gatheral, Lecture 1](https://www.maths.univ-evry.fr/pages_perso/crepey/Finance/lecture1_2004.pdf)). The ∂V/∂t there is unambiguously the partial at fixed S and v. With constant r, q and parameters, price depends on t and T only through τ, so ∂V/∂t|_{S,v} = −∂V/∂τ|_{S,v} exactly. That identity fails if r(t), q(t) or the model parameters are time-dependent, a case that some Heston extensions treat ([arXiv 0708.2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.2020)). The identity holds for your notebook as long as all inputs are constants.

Two traps are worth flagging. First, Gatheral then rewrites the PDE in forward log-moneyness x = ln(F/K) for the *undiscounted* value. His −∂C/∂τ there is taken at fixed x, not fixed S, and absorbs the rSV_S and −rV terms ([Gatheral §3.2](https://www.maths.univ-evry.fr/pages_perso/crepey/Finance/lecture1_2004.pdf)). A "theta" read off that form is not your spot-fixed theta. Second, Heston's θ is the long-run variance, a notational clash with the Greek ([optionsanalysissuite](https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/documentation/heston-theta-param)). Your be_vega = −κ(θ − v)·Vega uses θ in the parameter sense, and labelling it clearly avoids confusion.

## Theta pays for gamma, vanna, volga, drift and carry: the notebook's breakeven identity is the Heston PDE

In Black-Scholes, the PDE rewritten in Greeks is Hull's identity Θ + (r − q)SΔ + ½σ²S²Γ = rV. Its standard content is that for a delta-neutral book "theta and gamma have similar magnitudes and opposite signs" ([CCNY lecture notes](https://kfoster.ccny.cuny.edu/classes/spring2010/eco275/lecturenotes10.html)). (The equation number and page in Hull were not verified.) The P&L version is Carr and Madan's result, restated by Carr and Wu: buying an option at implied vol x and delta-hedging at x yields **PL = ∫ ½S²B_SS(S, x, u)(v_u − x²)du**. This is dollar-gamma-weighted realised-minus-implied variance, so theta funds gamma exactly at the implied-variance breakeven ([Carr & Wu 2016, eq. 19](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2018-09/CarrJournalofFinEconomics2016.pdf)). Bergomi's Chapter 1 (read via secondary notes) writes the BS PDE as dP/dt − rP + (r − q)SP_S = −½σ̂²S²P_SS. The financing and carry terms sit next to dP/dt, so the quantity that pays for gamma is the **financing-adjusted theta V_t − rV + (r − q)SΔ**, not bare V_t ([junfanz1 notes on Bergomi](https://junfanz1.github.io/blog/book%20notes%20series/Stochastic-Volatility-Modeling-Char-1-Introduction-Notes)).

Stochastic volatility adds three more terms. Carr and Wu's "fundamental PDE" in implied-vol coordinates reads −B_t = μB_σ + ½vS²B_SS + ρω√v S B_Sσ + ½ω²B_σσ. They name the terms "theta", "vega", "dollar gamma", "dollar vanna" and "volga", christen the framework the **Vega-Gamma-Vanna-Volga model**, and note that without the drift term it reduces to the earlier GVV model of Arslan et al. ([Carr & Wu 2016, eq. 6](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2018-09/CarrJournalofFinEconomics2016.pdf)). Bergomi's 2004 Risk article explains why this matters. Black-Scholes has "no theta matching ∂²P/∂σ̂²" and "no theta matching ∂²P/∂S∂σ̂", and "a suitable model needs to price in a theta to match these gammas" ([Bergomi, Smile Dynamics, Risk 2004](https://www.maths.univ-evry.fr/pages_perso/crepey/Finance/0904_tech_bergomi.pdf)). An online textbook chapter gives the Heston-coordinate version, with Θ = V_t, vega taken with respect to variance, and a delta-hedged P&L that collapses to 𝒱[dv − κ(θ − v)dt] once the PDE is substituted ([Lee, P&L Explanation Under Heston](https://sungchullee.github.io/financial_math_book_writing/ch16/greeks/pnl_explanation_under_heston)). It is a secondary, non-peer-reviewed source, and it omits the dividend leg on the hedge.

Rearranging the Heston PDE with a dividend yield gives your notebook's identity term for term (rearrangement derived from Gatheral's eq. 13 for this report):

| Notebook term | Formula | PDE origin and literature name |
|---|---|---|
| be_gamma | −½vS²Γ | Dollar gamma × instantaneous variance (Carr & Wu "dollar gamma") |
| be_vanna | −ρξvS·∂²V/∂S∂v | Cross-gamma × spot-variance covariance ("dollar vanna") |
| be_volga | −½ξ²v·∂²V/∂v² | Variance gamma × variance-of-variance ("volga") |
| be_vega | −κ(θ − v)·∂V/∂v | Vega × risk-neutral variance drift (Carr & Wu's μB_σ analogue) |
| be_rho | rV − (r − q)SΔ | Net carry: financing of premium minus stock carry net of dividends |
| Sum | = ∂V/∂t | Θ = rV − (r−q)SΔ − ½vS²Γ − ρξvSV_Sv − ½ξ²vV_vv − κ(θ−v)V_v |

Three precision points follow. First, the identity holds only if **Vega, Vanna and Volga are derivatives with respect to variance v**, not volatility √v. If the notebook reports vol-based Greeks anywhere, the conversions (V_v = V_σ/2σ, and V_vv = (V_σσ − V_σ/σ)/4σ²) must be applied before summing. Second, "be_rho" is really a **carry/financing term**, not rho (∂V/∂r). Renaming it be_carry would match how Bergomi's arrangement and the P&L derivations treat it. Third, the drift term depends on the coordinates. In forward-variance models the state ξ^T(t) is **driftless** under the pricing measure ([Bergomi, Smile Dynamics II](https://www.maths.univ-evry.fr/pages_perso/crepey/Finance/bergomi.pdf)), so theta there pays only for second-order terms. Heston's κ(θ − v)V_v is precisely the roll of the model's forward-variance curve, E[v_T] = θ + (v − θ)e^{−κ(T−t)}, at fixed v. That reconciliation is derived for this report and was not found stated in a source. The vega/drift term is therefore a feature of choosing v as the state variable, not a universal component of theta.

## Desk theta holds different things fixed than model theta

Practitioners agree that theta depends on what you freeze. QuantLib's maintainer computes numerical theta by moving the evaluation date and repricing. He shows three defensible ways to "keep everything else the same" (fixed quotes, fixed zero rates, fixed forwards) giving three different numbers, and concludes "I'm not sure there is one" correct theta ([Ballabio, Implementing QuantLib](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)). For options, the key choice is the vol coordinate. Vendor glossaries define theta at **constant implied volatility** and book the option's slide along a non-flat surface as a separate **vol-surface roll**, under sticky-strike or sticky-delta assumptions ([Convex Trade](https://convextrade.com/glossary/vol-surface-roll); [Convex Trade, term-structure roll](https://convextrade.com/glossary/implied-volatility-term-structure-roll)). Some traders add the two together as "effective theta = Black-Scholes theta + shadow theta" ([Moontower](https://moontowermeta.com/shadow-theta/)). Carr and Wu's theta is likewise ∂B/∂t at fixed S and fixed *implied* vol ([Carr & Wu 2016](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2018-09/CarrJournalofFinEconomics2016.pdf)).

Your Heston theta freezes instantaneous variance instead. Since Heston implied vol changes with τ even at fixed v, **model theta and implied-vol theta differ**, by roughly the roll-down of the model's own implied surface. Neither is wrong. They answer different questions: "what does the model say happens if nothing in the model's state moves?" versus "what happens if the quoted surface stays put?"

Carry is the second split. In the spot-fixed BS PDE, ∂V/∂t = rV − (r − q)SΔ − ½σ²S²Γ, so a spot-fixed theta includes carry and a forward-fixed theta does not (standard algebra, not a sourced convention). Wystup documents the practical consequence: a USD/JPY call where SuperDerivatives' one-day reprice gives theta of **−1,894 JPY** while Eikon gives **+4,064 JPY** for the same option. FX pricing also uses two clocks, vol time to expiry and discount time to delivery, which a holiday can move independently. He adds that the forward-curve part of theta is "the dominating one on average" ([Wystup](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)). Your notebook keeps carry inside theta (spot fixed) and splits it out as be_rho. That is the PDE-consistent choice, and it makes the gamma breakeven exact. Desks that quote "pure" theta are effectively reporting Θ − be_rho.

Day counts are the third split. Kohn and Allen say market convention is often per business day, dividing by "about 252" ([Kohn & Allen](https://math.nyu.edu/~kohn/derivative.securities/2012/section5.pdf)). Wikipedia says calendar vs business days "varies by personal choice" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_(finance))). A practitioner source calls ÷365 "the most common" display convention ([Ryan O'Connell](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/option-theta/), secondary). Your business-days/252 clock is a **trading-time clock** in which weekends carry zero variance time. It is defensible on the evidence: studies cited secondarily put Friday-to-Monday variance only about 19–22% above a normal day, not the roughly 3x that calendar time implies ([MenthorQ summary](https://menthorq.com/guide/the-hidden-problem-with-theta/), figures not checked against originals). Practitioners also observe that options "typically do not decay as much as the model's theta would predict" over weekends ([Moontower](https://moontowermeta.com/weekend-theta/)). Many FX and equity desks use a compromise: weekends and holidays get fractional weight and event days get extra weight ([Moontower variance schedule](https://notion.moontowermeta.com/a-variance-schedule); [GlobalCapital](https://www.globalcapital.com/article/28mwtvkodfvd095dm36yo/derivatives/pricing-short-dated-options)). Two consistency requirements follow for your notebook. First, v, θ, κ and ξ must be calibrated on the same 252-day year. Second, r and q, normally quoted on calendar-day bases, must be rescaled to that clock or discounting will be subtly misstated. The latter is Wystup's two-clock issue in another guise.

## Multi-day theta is full repricing on a frozen state, and it equals the integral exactly

Practitioners compute theta by repricing, not by differentiating. Wystup describes moving "the trade date ... to the next day, keeping all other market and contract values the same" ([Wystup](https://mathfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-01-LongTheta-ShortTheta.pdf)). QuantLib moves the evaluation date and reports new price minus old price as theta per day, without annualising ([Ballabio](https://implementingquantlib.substack.com/p/different-approaches-to-numerical)). Regulators take the same view. The EBA's backtesting rules require the "passage of time (i.e. the theta effect)" in actual P&L, treated consistently with the expected shortfall calculation "where the portfolio is assumed to be static" ([EBA RTS 2020/02](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library//EBA-RTS-2020-02%20Final%20draft%20RTS%20on%20Backtesting%20and%20PLA%20requirements.pdf)). Your V(τ − h) − V(τ) with S and v frozen is this standard full-revaluation "slide" theta, specialised to the Heston state.

The link to PDE theta is the fundamental theorem of calculus, a derivation not found stated in any source but exact. With the state x = (S, v, r, q, parameters) held fixed, **V(τ − h, x) − V(τ, x) = ∫₀ʰ ∂_tV(t + s, x) ds**. Because the breakeven identity holds pointwise at every (τ, x), integrating each instantaneous breakeven along the same frozen path gives horizon breakevens that **sum exactly to the slide theta**. That is your notebook's construction. Up to quadrature error this is an identity, so a residual between the summed integrals and the repriced difference measures numerical error, not model error. The slide differs from h·Θ(τ) by the curvature of theta in time (½h²∂²V/∂t² + …), which matters near expiry and wherever the clock jumps.

Two caveats limit how this number can be read. First, the frozen-state integral is not the integral of theta along the *realised* path, which is what enters the hedged-P&L identity ∫(Θ + ½ΓvS² + …)dt. Over multiple days spot and variance move, so the horizon gamma breakeven computed at frozen S is a forecast of the theta budget, not an attribution of realised P&L. Second, the slide only matches PDE theta when the frozen coordinates are the model's state variables. Freezing implied vol per strike, or the forward instead of spot, gives a different and equally conventional number. For P&L explain, practitioner sources note that whether time moves first or last in a sequential explain is "often the biggest methodological decision" ([Atoti PnL Explain docs](https://docs.activeviam.com/products/solutions/mra/6.0/online-help/calculations/pnl-explain.html), quote via search summary, unverified).

## Conclusion

Your notebook does not need correcting; it needs labels. Every element (theta as ∂V/∂t at fixed S, v, r, q and parameters, the five-term breakeven identity, and the frozen-state slide with integrated breakevens) follows from the Heston PDE and agrees with the verified references. The more useful insight is that each choice you made is one point in a space of conventions, and each alternative maps to a specific adjustment of your output. Subtracting be_rho gives a "pure" forward-fixed theta. Adding the model's implied-vol roll-down gives something close to a sticky-strike desk theta. Switching to forward-variance coordinates absorbs be_vega into the roll. Rescaling from a 252-day to a 365-day or weighted clock changes per-day numbers and weekend behaviour.

The weakest link is not the theory but the plumbing. Greeks with respect to variance rather than volatility, a single consistent year basis for v, κ, ξ, r and q, and the time-homogeneity that licenses ∂_t = −∂_τ all have to hold for the identity to close. Stating those conventions alongside every theta figure is the step the literature most consistently recommends and most systems most often omit.
